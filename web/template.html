    How it is counted

    Every 2 minutes SNCF publishes a real-time feed of every train running in France (TGV, Intercités, TER), with each train's delay at each station. This map uses an archive of that feed, compared with the timetable SNCF published for the same day.

    Each stop a train makes at a station counts once, in the station's département. It is late if the train arrived more than 5 minutes after the timetable (at its first station, departed), which is SNCF's own definition. The feed gives delays in whole minutes, so that means 6 minutes or more. It is cancelled if the whole train or that stop was cancelled. A stop is only counted once the feed shows the train has actually reached it. Predictions for stops still ahead are never used. A train that runs late from Lyon onwards counts as late at every stop after Lyon, in each département it serves.

    Coverage is the share of the timetable's train stops that the feed confirmed. A département's day is left out when its coverage is under , so gaps in the source never look like punctual trains.

    Not included

    Paris-region Transilien and RER trains, which are a separate network. Extra trains not in the timetable. Cancellations announced before the day's timetable was published, since those trains are already gone from it (this matters on planned strike days). Days before . Earlier archives exist, but checked against SNCF's published figures they under-reported disruption by 4 to 7 points, so they are left out.
